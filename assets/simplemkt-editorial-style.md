# SimpleMkt Editorial Art Direction

When using the SimpleMkt default, apply this visual language to the cover and every content image. The cover and content images are one series, but they have different jobs: the cover is a PPT-cover presentation and content images are explanatory diagrams. There is no landscape photography, pale-green outer frame, paper tint, warm color cast, or generic SVG/wireframe treatment.

## Canvas

- Use the fixed canvas dimensions required by the package.
- Use one opaque, pure solid white `#FFFFFF` editorial surface across the full canvas. Material detail belongs only inside explanatory elements and must never tint the canvas.
- Keep all titles, labels, arrows, and notes inside a generous 5% safe area. The top-right Logo overlay zone is stricter for text and essential diagram semantics, but it is not a pre-rendered white reserve.

## Shared editorial language

- Use one high-contrast Chinese Songti-like editorial-serif family for every visible Chinese string. On the fixed 1200×675 body-figure canvas, set the core judgment at 52px semibold in exact `#1A6B3A`; set its one-line subtitle at 28px regular in exact `#6B6B6B`; set structural headings at 32px medium and structural labels at 28px regular, both in exact `#141414`; set the lower-left disclosure or note at 16px italic in exact `#8C8C8C`. English technical identifiers use restrained upright `Inter Variable` only when a clear Chinese label is unavailable.
- Use a light editorial sketch language: direct ink, airy pencil or engraving contours, restrained cross-hatching, thin printed labels, and small circular anchors. Select the source-derived semantic form before its material treatment: directly depict its objects, actors, actions, states, positions, and relationships, then render that form with the shared sketch treatment. Paper is an optional semantic material, not a default visual primitive: use an excerpt sheet only when text, a document, a claim, or a source is itself part of the explanation; use an outlined strip for a stage and an outlined field for a decision, boundary, or contained scope only when those forms match the semantic role. Do not force unrelated meanings into document pages or paper cards.
- Make every core semantic role depict source-specific information before labels explain it. Show a concrete object, action, state change, position, relationship, or evidence detail when the source provides one. Labels clarify the depiction; they must not substitute for it. Readable fictional microcopy is permitted when it clarifies the explanation and follows the disclosure rule below.
- Treat the selected visual grammar as the organizing structure, not as a substitute for the source meaning. Populate it with source-specific semantic elements in the same editorial sketch treatment. A pencil or engraving depiction may directly carry a source-derived object, action, state, or position; it need not be a separate illustrative subject or metaphor. Every depicted element must follow ordinary physical, causal, and semantic expectations. Never add a detached decorative subject or a large shaded illustration.
- Keep every element nearly flat and visually grounded. Allow subtle white-paper grain and restrained straight, cut, or softly irregular edges on semantic paper surfaces; do not apply paper texture to the canvas or force every element to be paper. In one content image, paper may support a source-derived role but must not become the repeated default container for the primary semantic groups. Reject thick torn edges, decorative paper stacks, and visible drop shadows. Use no shadow by default; only when separation is genuinely necessary, use one consistent near-imperceptible lift—at most a 1px-equivalent elevation with a diffuse neutral shadow below 1% opacity. Use thin, low-contrast 1–1.5px-equivalent forest-green or light-gray linework. Construction lines may frame the subject, terminate a leader, or clarify a position inside the core diagram.
- Treat the top-right Logo zone as a precise post-generation overlay, not a white block. Keep title, subtitle, label, connector, and essential diagram content out of its coordinates; allow the ordinary white canvas or non-semantic background texture to continue beneath the later Logo. Do not render a model-made wordmark.

## Cover presentation

- Treat the cover as a normal PPT cover, not as a content image: compose its approved title at prominent cover scale with one concise visual direction in a calm, continuous title-safe zone. The direction may be one vivid metaphor or one restrained abstract composition that communicates the article's core idea; keep it simple, with no multi-object scene or explanatory-diagram complexity. Deliberate title line breaks are allowed only for readability.
- Prioritize elements that show content being edited: article pages, paragraphs and columns, margins, underlines, circles, brackets, annotation leaders, focus fields, tabs, bookmarks, folded corners, binding lines, anchors, fine connectors, and light relationship structures. Render them with pencil, ink, restrained hatching, and local paper grain.
- Make those elements serve the title. When they are insufficient, choose a source-related object or restrained abstract form; do not use natural scenery, plants, animals, or people as visual filler.
- Render no text other than the approved title by default. Subtitles, labels, source excerpts, data, notes, microcopy, and other title-external text require an explicit request.
- The cover does not need a visual-grammar topology, explanatory labels, relation words, short explanation, or evidence note. It must not turn the article into a flowchart or a generic report slide.

## Content-image composition

- Maintain `core judgment → short explanation → one dominant grammar structure populated with source-specific meaning → evidence note only when needed`, generous whitespace, and one dominant relationship. Preserve the selected visual grammar and source topology without reducing the source meaning to generic geometry.
- The planned `core_judgment` is the content image's centered title, not a small corner heading. On the 1200×675 canvas, fix the centered title/subtitle band to `x=600`, `y=76–176`: place the 52px semibold title and its 28px subtitle inside that band, leave a 16px gap below the Logo overlay zone ending at `y=60`, and use an invisible centered content stage at `x=54–1146`, `y=190–560`. Every semantic object, group heading, label, connector, anchor, and optional semantic material must remain wholly inside this stage; it is a layout boundary, not a default drawn frame.
- Use exactly one core structure under that judgment. Build the smallest sufficient explanatory system, normally two to four primary semantic groups rather than a hard cap on all elements. Each primary group may contain the internal details needed to visualize the source meaning—such as an object, action, state, position, highlighted relation, or compact substructure. Remove unrelated decoration, but do not compress away necessary semantics. Use a source excerpt only when text or a document is itself relevant; do not add a separate narrative scene or metaphor by default.
- Allow paragraphs, multi-line excerpts, or fictional body copy when text, a document, or a claim itself helps explain the relationship. Keep them to the necessary local content: they must not occupy the primary content area or become the image's main reading task. Make relationships, objects, positions, and actions carry the visual focus. Apply the lower-left disclosure rule to non-factual copy.
- When the plan requires an illustrative disclosure, render it in the lower-left safe area using the fixed 16px italic `#8C8C8C` note style. It is a disclosure, not a centered caption or diagram component.
