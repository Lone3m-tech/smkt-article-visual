# SimpleMkt Editorial Art Direction

When using the SimpleMkt default, apply this visual language to the cover and every body figure. The cover and body figures are one series, but they have different jobs: the cover is a PPT-cover presentation and the body figures are explanatory diagrams. There is no landscape photography, pale-green outer frame, paper tint, warm color cast, or generic SVG/wireframe treatment.

## Canvas

- Finalize every cover and body figure at the fixed canvas dimensions reported by `scripts/place_brand_wordmark.py --print-contract`.
- Use one opaque, pure solid white `#FFFFFF` editorial surface across the full canvas. Do not use ivory, cream, warm paper, yellowed paper, beige, gray, color cast, texture, gradient, or an off-white **background**. Do not add an outer frame, scenic border, colored edge, shadow, rounded card, glass surface, or product UI.
- Keep all titles, labels, arrows, and notes inside a generous 5% safe area. The top-right Logo reserve is stricter and has priority.

Pure white is a canvas rule, not an instruction to make the diagram flat. Material detail is permitted only inside the explanatory elements and must never tint the full canvas.

## Shared editorial language

- Use one high-contrast Chinese Songti-like editorial-serif family for every visible Chinese string: core judgment, subtitle, column heading, label, note, and disclosure. On the fixed 1200×675 body-figure canvas, set the core judgment at 52px semibold in exact `#1A6B3A`; set its one-line subtitle at 28px regular in exact `#6B6B6B`; set structural headings at 32px medium and paper-node labels at 28px regular, both in exact `#141414`; set the lower-left disclosure or note at 16px italic in exact `#8C8C8C`. Do not substitute a larger content label, smaller note, or second Chinese font family. English technical identifiers use only restrained upright `Inter Variable` (never a handwritten or script face), and are omitted when a clear Chinese label is available.
- Constrain every non-text content element to this editorial vocabulary: use a small excerpt sheet for a source or claim; use one straight-edged narrow paper strip for a process stage, judgment, or output; use one cut-paper field for a decision, boundary, or contained scope; use direct ink marks, hatch, or a circular anchor for a position or measured change. A source/claim sheet uses supplied lines when available; when a compact illustrative excerpt is clearer, it may use readable fictional microcopy under the disclosure rule below. Reuse the same primitive for the same role within one figure. Every core element must visibly carry the role-specific information it represents: a source/claim sheet exposes its relevant lines or highlight, a relationship exposes its selected nodes and links, an explanatory figure exposes its framed or annotated relation, and a reader takeaway exposes the resolved reading outcome. Do not use an empty paper shape, an undifferentiated ruled sheet, or a repeated strip whose label is its only semantic content. Do not invent icons, machines, containers, abstract patterns, or other visual objects outside this vocabulary.
- Use one grammar-matched editorial engraving or pencil-sketch subject derived from the article's stated relation or concrete source detail. Use controlled contour lines and restrained cross-hatching or print grain. Do not default to botanical, animal, or generic object metaphors; introduce one only when it is supplied by the article or necessary to preserve the stated structure. The subject must carry the explanation, never act as detached decoration. Build its non-text content from the fixed editorial vocabulary above; use direct printed labels with thin leader lines and small circular anchor points by default. Keep all paper primitives in one family: white, subtly fibrous, straight- or cut-edge, never rounded, card-like, panel-like, sticker-like, or UI-like. Give every independent paper primitive the same deliberately faint lift: approximately 4–6px-equivalent elevation with one diffuse neutral shadow of approximately 12–16px-equivalent blur at 4–6% opacity. Do not apply shadows to the engraved subject, direct labels, leaders, or construction lines. Linework is predominantly thin and low-contrast: hairline or fine 1–1.5px-equivalent strokes, forest green at roughly 45–60% visual intensity or light gray. Allow only small local weight variation for clarity; do not use thick outlines, bold arrows, heavy frames, or dark connector routes. Permit pale construction lines, alignment grids, and measurement-like guide marks only inside the core diagram when they visibly frame the subject, terminate a leader, or clarify position; do not use them as page decoration, crop marks, or floating ornament. Keep the background pure `#FFFFFF`; material cues are clipped to the diagram itself and must not tint the canvas. Do not add decorative registration marks, corner crosses, floating strokes, isolated color blocks, or ornamental arrows; every visible element must communicate a planned label, relation, or structure.
- Use the packaged `assets/logo/simplemkt-logo-demo.png`; never ask the model to render or imitate the wordmark. Before generation, read the fixed contract from `scripts/place_brand_wordmark.py --print-contract` and reserve its exact top-origin protected rectangle as unoccupied pure white; inject those bounds into the compiled prompt. The reserve belongs to the Logo only: title, subtitle, label, connector, and diagram bounds must not intersect it. If a title needs room, reflow or reduce it before generation rather than encroaching on the reserve.

## Cover presentation

- Treat the cover as a normal PPT cover, not as a body figure: compose a large, phone-readable title and one dominant visual metaphor in a calm, continuous title-safe zone. The title is the article H1 exactly; it may use deliberate line breaks for readability but must never be abbreviated, rewritten, reordered, or replaced.
- The title may retain its prominent cover scale. Do not apply the body-figure title cap to it.
- The cover does not need a visual-grammar topology, explanatory labels, relation words, short explanation, or evidence note. It must not turn the article into a flowchart or a generic report slide.

## Body-figure composition

- Maintain `core judgment → short explanation → diagram → evidence note when needed`, generous whitespace, and one dominant relationship. Preserve the selected visual grammar and source topology.
- The planned `visible_title` is the body figure's centered core judgment, not a small corner heading. On the 1200×675 canvas, fix the centered title/subtitle band to `x=600`, `y=76–176`: place the 52px semibold title and its 28px subtitle inside that band, leave a 16px gap below the Logo reserve ending at `y=60`, and begin the diagram at or below `y=200`. Do not left-align, top-left-align, or reflow this band around the diagram. The 32px structural heading, 28px paper-node label, and 16px lower-left note remain subordinate, and the whole band remains clearly smaller than a cover title.
- Use exactly one core structure under that judgment. It may contain a linked, detailed reading chain—for example `source paragraph or claim → selected change → explanatory graphic → reader conclusion`—when that chain is the chosen grammar. Do not flatten this chain into anonymous placeholder lines, generic tokens, or visually empty paper nodes whose label is doing all the explanatory work.
- Do not make decorative art, a repeated prose block, an example chart, and a conclusion card into multiple equal centers. Detail is welcome when every component is visibly connected in the one reading path and serves the selected visual grammar and reader takeaway. A sketch motif may be the central explanatory subject only when direct labels and leaders connect its concrete features to the intended relation.
- When any non-factual illustrative text, example, claim, number, date, percentage, source-like chart, or other example is necessary, place the exact note `图中示例仅为解释用途，并非事实` in the lower-left safe area at the fixed 16px italic `#8C8C8C` note style. A readable illustrative paragraph is permitted; it must not masquerade as a real quotation, source, or fact. The note is a disclosure, not a centered caption or a diagram component.
- Do not use a dashboard, product UI, generic report, glossy 3D, corporate vector template, neon effects, random circles joined by arrows, empty rounded rectangles, outline-only boxes, wireframe, or flat SVG-like diagrams.
